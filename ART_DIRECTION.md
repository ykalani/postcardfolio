# Art direction: a living brush painting

Every addition to this project should feel made by the same painter, with the same brushes and pigments as the existing landscapes. This applies to new scenes, objects, atmospheric effects, animation, and interface treatments. Preserve the established paper navigation and readable typography when adding UI.

## The visual language

The landscapes have the character of expressive plein-air painting: opaque gouache-like color planes, the weight of a loaded flat brush, and broken dry-brush edges. Forms emerge from overlapping strokes of pigment. Brushwork should be visible at normal viewing size, with enough irregularity to suggest a hand pulling bristles across a textured surface.

- **Build with paint planes.** Use broad, irregular marks to establish mass and shape, then a few smaller accents to describe important edges. Keep gaps and broken coverage where underlying pigment can show through.
- **Give strokes direction.** Follow the thing being painted: the slope of rock, the growth of foliage, the current of water or lava, and the drift of smoke. Marks should describe material and form.
- **Keep a deliberate palette.** Use a small family of distinct pigment values with warm and cool relationships. Reserve the lightest accents for focal areas. Let cooler, quieter distance recede behind richer foreground paint.
- **Match the existing stroke scale.** New marks should have comparable length, breadth, spacing, and edge roughness to nearby paint. Preserve recognizable silhouettes without filling them with photographic microdetail.
- **Vary edges with purpose.** Mix firm structural edges with broken, lost edges. Texture comes from the marks themselves; an overlay of grain cannot turn a smooth effect into brushwork.

## Paint that moves

Animate the painted material itself. Reuse painted cutouts, source-colored marks, and masks tied to the landscape. Move coherent patches of pigment, let their intensity breathe, and keep sources, banks, roots, and attachments grounded. Texture should persist as it travels rather than being randomized every frame.

Lava should read as shifting ember-red and orange paint with broken pale-hot dabs, moving through the established fractures. Avoid smooth round-ended tubes, curling outlined worms, repeated cellular shapes, or continuous neon filaments. Smoke should retain layered bristle marks as it expands and drifts.

Avoid glossy vector shapes, airbrushed gradients, photographic inserts, uniform particle sprites, and dense procedural detail that hides the brushwork. Stronger motion or brightness must still preserve the painting's material character.

## Review standard

Inspect the addition within the whole composition on desktop and mobile, both paused and over a short motion sequence. It should belong to the existing painting in palette, stroke scale, texture, depth, and rhythm. If it reads as a separate visual effect laid over the scene, revise the marks before adding more detail.
